    06:14:55 GST · a real pass

    Sentinel-1D,
    693 km up.

    It doesn't need the ship's permission. It sends its own radar pulses, so it sees in the dark and through cloud.

    One look, 250 km wide

    Every hull on the water bounces the pulse back. The scene goes down to a ground station, then into the Copernicus archive, free for anyone.

    08:01 · radar can't say what it is

    So the pipeline asks the nearest buoy.

    A radar return says something 189 m long is there. It can't read a name, see a slick, or listen for a radio. Buoy B-07 is moored 3.5 km away. The task goes out over a satellite IoT link: a few hundred bytes, no mobile coverage needed.

    What the buoy sees: a tanker, nothing on AIS, sitting in a dark, glassy patch of sea.

    08:04 · two sources, one alert

    Radar found it. The buoy confirmed it.

    Each source alone is a maybe. Together they make a lead worth a patrol boat: a ship that is there, not broadcasting, and leaving oil behind it.

    A lead, not a verdict. A person decides what happens next.
